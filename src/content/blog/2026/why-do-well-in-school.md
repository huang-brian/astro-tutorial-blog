If I had kids, I'd tell them to do well in school. It set them up for success in the future. Helps them get into a good college, and help them land a good job. Right? This is what we tell our kids and what we tell ourselves as parents. But is this really true? I think what we're asking for when we ask our kids to do well in school is we're asking our kids to be engaged. We want our kids to be engaged in school, and we we see our kids' report card with all A's and B's we know that they're doing so. When our kids bring back a report card with all A's and B's,  we can relax knowing that they're doing well. It's an easy indicator for parents to know that their kids are doing well. 

I argue that a progress report is __not__ a good indicator that kids are doing well. 

When we tell kids to do well in school, we're asking for them to be engaged. A report card is simply a measure of how well a student is engaged in a school environment. It does __not__ mean the student is engaged with their own life of rht lives of the people and the world around them, which, I argue, is more important.

When I was in grade school my mom wanted me to get good grades. (I think that's obvious.) I did get good grades throughout elementary school, and then my grades dropped precipitously through middle and high school, contributing to lots of tension between me and my mom. Even though I didn't earn good grades in high school, I still wanted to enroll in honors and AP classes, because most of my friends were in these classes and I felt like I learned more in them. The students in honors classes weren't as rowdy as the regular classes, and the teachers were generally more interesting to listen to. I took AP classes even though I knew I'd probably earn less than a B (for lack of doing homework). (Homework is hard man and my brain doesn't want to think that hard. Homework in regular classes is so much easier [or the grading standards are so much lower]). 

What does it mean to be engaged? Listening, asking questions, and responding to challenges and solving problems.

## Listening
You can't be engaged if you're not listening. By listening, I mean hearing what other people are saying, listening to the birds and the sounds of the world around you, observing your surroundings. It means taking in information so that you're aware of what's happening around you. It means cultivating a habit of awareness. Videogames, tv, and social media feeds give us an abundant feed of inrmation, but true awareness is inhabited in your body and performed through the five senses, By observing what's happening in the world around us, only then are we ready to respond to life's challenges.

## Ask questions
Now that we know what's happening on the world around us, we're ready to ask questions. Why is the world this way? Was it always this way? Where did this come from and where do they want to go? By asking questions, we begin to envision change and find ways to deep our understanding. 

## Responding to challenges and solving problems

After listening and asking questions, it is time to act. If we identify a problem that needs fixing, we can fix it. If there's an obstacle stopping you from living fully, take steps to overcome it. If there's somewhere you want to be, find the first step to get there and take it. If there's something you think people should know, go say it.
